## C1. The HITS description matches networkx 3.6.1, but the evaluation ran on networkx 3.7, whose default is power iteration; the 8 convergence failures behind Ch7's excluded SBOMs come from that switch
- Verdict: conceded
- Evidence:
  1. Which version ran. The pipeline starts the backend with `config.SBOM_LENS_VENV / "bin" / "uvicorn"` (`sbom-lens-eval/pipeline/lens.py:46`, `config.py:19`), so it uses `sbom-lens/venv`. `work/backend.log` confirms this: its warnings come from `/home/inasc/projects/sbom-lens/venv/lib/python3.14/site-packages/powerlaw/...`. That venv holds only `networkx-3.7.dist-info`, written 2026-09-30 16:44 +0100. The run started 21:45 UTC (22:45 local, `data/run.json`), and the first result file is dated 22:59 local. No other networkx install exists on the machine (a `find /` turns up only this venv). There is no lock file. `backend/requirements.txt` gives `networkx>=3.6.1`, and `run.json` records node, cdxgen, Python and the commits, but not the Python libraries.
  2. What each version does. In 3.6.1, `hits(G, max_iter=100, tol=1e-8, nstart=None, normalized=True)` has no `method` argument. It calls `sp.sparse.linalg.svds(A, k=1, ...)`, sets `a = vt`, `h = A @ a`, and normalises both to sum one. This is exactly the thesis text. In 3.7 (`hits_alg.py:9-96`) the signature adds `method="power_iteration"` as the default. `_hits_power_iteration` (:99-142) starts from `h = 1/N`, iterates `a = h @ A; h = A @ a; h /= h.max()`, stops when `sum|h - h_last| < tol`, and raises `PowerIterationFailedConvergence` after `max_iter` sweeps. The SVD path survives as `method="svd"` (:145-169), with the 3.6.1 code. The 3.7 release notes list "BUG: Use power iteration as the default HITS implementation" (PR #8620). The PR's motivation is issue #6289 (SVD sometimes returned negative scores). `impact.py:72,101` call `nx.hits(G, max_iter=200)` with no `method`.
  3. The stored results come from power iteration. In 8 SBOMs every stored `global-analysis` authority is 0: CopyTranslator, stackedit, CyberChef, lowlighter/metrics, marktext, parse-server, typeorm, wekan. Under 3.7 power iteration with `max_iter=200`, all 8 raise, and none of the other 82 does. On the 82 that converge, my recomputed power-iteration authorities equal the stored values exactly after 6-decimal rounding (82/82). The SVD path converges on all 8. Five repeated runs agree to within 3e-13, and the result matches a dense `eigh(AᵀA)` to within 6e-14. Real networkx 3.6.1 `nx.hits(G, max_iter=200)` also converges on them; I checked CopyTranslator, typeorm and wekan. The SVD path cannot give all zeros, so the stored zeros can only come from the 3.7 default.
  4. Why power iteration fails. The leading singular values of A are close together: the relative gap (σ1−σ2)/σ1 runs from 6.1e-5 (typeorm) to 4.7e-2 (marktext). With 200 iterations the L1 change was still 1.2e-8 to 2.9e-2. Convergence comes after 202 (marktext), 239, 298, 487, 519, 802 and 4,465 (CyberChef) iterations, and not within 20,000 for typeorm. The cause is the default switch plus a 200-iteration limit that is too low for small gaps. It is not oscillation, and HITS itself is not undefined on these graphs.
  5. Effect on Ch7. Among the 8, vulnerable components take 15, 2, 7, 17, 10, 31, 2 and 9 distinct authority values under SVD, so the critic's "2 to 31" is correct. All 8 get a defined τ_b. I re-ran `analyse.rq2` with the SVD authorities substituted. τ_b for authority is then defined in 87 SBOMs instead of 79. The median stays at −0.004 (reported as 0.00). The counts go from 13 at ≥ 0.2 and 14 at ≤ −0.2 to 15 and 15, and |τ_b| < 0.2 goes from 52 to 57. Zero top-k overlaps fall from 64 to 61. The qualitative RQ2 finding ("essentially unrelated") does not change.
- Viva defence: the convergence failure and its handling are real behaviour of the evaluated system, and the thesis disclosed the 11 exclusions. What was wrong is the account of the algorithm and of why it failed. The cause is a dependency default that changed between releases, with a 200-iteration limit too low for graphs whose two leading singular values are nearly equal. Under the 3.6.1 SVD behaviour, the 8 SBOMs converge, and adding them leaves the RQ2 conclusion unchanged (median τ_b 0.00; 15/15 instead of 13/14 beyond ±0.2).
- Minimal thesis fix: the author must choose one of two routes.
  (a) Text only: Ch6:32, "NetworkX obtains the authority vector as the leading singular vector ... over the graph." → "In the NetworkX version used for the evaluation (3.7), this runs power iteration: starting from uniform hub scores, it alternates \(\mathbf{a} \leftarrow \mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{h}\) and \(\mathbf{h} \leftarrow \mathbf{A}\mathbf{a}\), rescaling \(\mathbf{h}\), until \(\mathbf{h}\) changes by less than \(10^{-8}\). The limit is the leading singular-vector pair of \(\mathbf{A}\), i.e. the principal eigenvector of \(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\), and both vectors are then normalised to sum to one. Convergence is slow when the two largest singular values of \(\mathbf{A}\) are close; if it has not converged after 200 iterations, all scores are reported as zero." Ch7:109 → "For HITS authority, 11 SBOMs could not be ranked. In 8, power iteration had not converged after 200 iterations (the two largest singular values of their adjacency matrices differ by less than 5\%), and SBOM Lens then reports every score as zero. In the other 3, HITS converged but every vulnerable component has authority zero (\autoref{sec:hits})." Add one sentence to Ch7 §7.6 (threats): "NetworkX 3.6.1 computed HITS by SVD, which converges on all 8; with those values, authority is defined for 87 SBOMs and the median \(\tau_b\) remains 0.00." Ch7:169: add "NetworkX 3.7" to the recorded versions.
  (b) Pin `method="svd"` (or raise `max_iter`), rerun, and keep the SVD text. The 3 constant-authority SBOMs of C2 remain in either case.

## C3. HITS authority does not "rank libraries by how central they are": on these DAGs it localises on a small block, and most components get an authority of (near) zero
- Verdict: conceded (the mechanism can be stated more precisely than the critic does)
- Evidence: Over the 82 SBOMs where HITS converges, the share of nodes with non-zero rounded authority has median 0.46 (min 0.07), as the critic says. The share above 1e-12 is about the same (median 0.52, min 0.07), so rounding is not the cause. Half of the vulnerable components score 0 in the median SBOM (median share 0.50). The mechanism is reducibility. AᵀA links two components only if they share a direct consumer (co-citation). On these graphs that co-citation graph splits into many blocks (median 60 blocks besides the root). The principal eigenvector lives on one block and is exactly zero elsewhere. In 74/90 SBOMs, the non-zero authorities are exactly the block that holds the largest-authority node. The thesis already shows this effect in its own example: Ch3's log4j table reports HITS authority "0.167, tied with the application's five other direct dependencies (all other components: 0)".
- Viva defence: authority is used only as a second, contrasting ordering in RQ2, beside blast radius, which is defined everywhere. The RQ2 conclusion does not depend on authority being a complete ordering. But the sentence as written claims more than HITS gives on dependency DAGs.
- Minimal thesis fix: Ch6:34, "it ranks libraries by how central they are to the dependency structure, weighting ..." → "it ranks libraries by how central they are within the most strongly linked part of the dependency structure, weighting .... On dependency graphs this part is often small: components are linked in \(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\) only when they share a direct consumer, and the authority vector is zero outside the dominant group of such components (in the evaluation, half of the components of a typical graph). Authority therefore orders only that group; the remaining components are tied at zero." Optionally add to Ch7:107: "about half of the vulnerable components of the median SBOM are tied at zero authority".

## C6. The small-world reading confuses undirected hops with exposure: a typical component is depended on, transitively, by about 1 % of the graph
- Verdict: partly rebutted

- Minimal thesis fix: Ch6:178, "a vulnerability introduced anywhere is structurally close to everything." → "in the undirected projection, every component is close to every other. ## C16. Wang et al. model discrete-time probabilities under a mean-field approximation, not "rates" of an exact SIS process
- Verdict: rebutted
- Evidence: The thesis does not say "exact"; it says "at rate β ... at rate δ". The source uses the same words. Chakrabarti et al. 2008 (the TISSEC extension, §3) say: "We assume a universal infection rate β for each edge connected to an infected node and a virus death rate δ for each infected node". On the discrete steps they say: "Our model works with small discrete time-steps Δt, with Δt → 0. This is only for ease of exposition; the same results also hold for the continuous case." "Mean-field" is the wrong label: the paper contrasts itself with the "'mean-field' approach" of Pastor-Satorras & Vespignani, "where all graphs with a given degree distribution are considered equal". What the paper does use is an "independence" assumption to replace the 2^N-state Markov chain.
- Viva defence: the thesis's wording follows the source's own terminology. The threshold is used only as a static index (:126).
- Minimal thesis fix: none required. Optional precision: after "\citep{Wang2003}" add "(under an assumption that the infection states of neighbours are independent)".
