## C1. The maximum exponent "3.00" is the `powerlaw` package's default upper bound, not a measured value; 8 of the 87 fits sit on it
- Verdict: conceded (one sub-claim overstated)
- Evidence:
  1. The bound. `powerlaw/distributions.py:1771` (installed 2.0.0) sets `Power_Law.DEFAULT_PARAMETER_RANGES = {'alpha': [0, 3]}`. `initialize_parameter_ranges` (:417-477) copies the default when `parameter_ranges` is not given. `fit()` passes the bounds to `scipy.optimize.minimize` (:692). The 2.0.0 documentation states this default (`docs/source/tutorials/ranges_and_constraints.rst:23-31` in the sdist from PyPI: "For a power law, the default parameter ranges are: {'alpha': [0, 3]}"). The bound is new in 2.0.0. In powerlaw 1.5 (wheel from PyPI, `powerlaw.py:1141-1142`), `Power_Law._in_standard_parameter_range` is only `self.alpha > 1`, with no upper bound. The sdist has no changelog entry that announces the change. 2.0.0 was uploaded on 2026-01-02. The tool calls `powerlaw.Fit(degrees, discrete=True, verbose=False)` without `parameter_ranges` (`backend/routers/graph_analysis.py:128`).
  2. The 8 fits. I rebuilt each graph from the SBOM's `dependencies` and checked that its in-degree histogram equals the stored `logLogData`. A default refit then reproduces all 87 stored (α, x_min) pairs exactly. The 8 SBOMs the critic names have α = `2.999999999999999`. For each of them, every x_min candidate is pinned at 3 and marked invalid (`xmin_fitting_results['valid_fits']` all False). `Fit.find_xmin` therefore sets `noise_flag` and emits `UserWarning: No valid values for xmin found.` (`fitting.py:512-528`). It then falls back to the smallest KS distance over the invalid candidates. The package documents this warning as meaning "none of the possible xmin values yielded a successful fit" (`docs/source/tutorials/warnings.rst:169-174`). So by the package's own rule, these 8 are failed fits. The backend never checks `noise_flag` and returns them as exponents. With the stored x_min and `parameter_ranges={'alpha': [1, 10]}`, their MLEs are 3.029 to 3.386, exactly the critic's values. The other 79 fits are unchanged.
  3. Effect on x_min selection. The bound also affects the other graphs, because a candidate whose α hits 3 is excluded from the x_min search (`fitting.py:448`, `in_range() and not noise_flag`). In 75 of the 79 other graphs, at least one candidate was excluded this way (`cand.py`). With the range widened and x_min chosen freely, x_min changes in 12 of 87 graphs, and α exceeds 3 in 17 (`fit_sum.py`), with a maximum of 6.72. The large values come from small tails: 6.72 is on 8 components at x_min 4 (google-webfonts-helper), and 4.71 on 20 at x_min 4 (audiobookshelf).
  4. Overstated sub-claim. "The upper part of the distribution (Q3 2.84) [is an] artefact of the bound" is not right. With the stored x_min and the bound lifted, Q1, median and Q3 are unchanged (2.50 / 2.62 / 2.84), and only the maximum moves (3.00 to 3.39). The 8 capped values already sit above Q3, so they cannot move it. Only a free x_min re-selection moves Q3 (to 2.93) and the median (to 2.67). The figure claim is right: the last of the 15 histogram bins (2.94–3.00) is the tallest, with 12 SBOMs, and 8 of those 12 are the capped fits.
- Viva defence: "The fit uses `powerlaw` with its defaults. Version 2.0.0 introduced an undocumented upper bound of 3 on α, and the package itself flags 8 of the 87 fits as failed. Without those 8, the 79 valid fits lie between 2.09 and 2.99 (median 2.59). Lifting the bound at the same x_min leaves the median and the interquartile range unchanged and puts the 8 fits between 3.03 and 3.39. The qualitative claim, a heavy right tail with a few highly used components, does not depend on the cap. The claim that every exponent lies in the scale-free range does."
- Minimal thesis fix: Ch7:77 "The fitted exponents are tightly clustered around \(\alpha = 2.62\) (interquartile range 2.50--2.84) and all lie between 2.09 and 3.00, inside the range typical of scale-free networks" → "The median fitted exponent is \(\alpha = 2.62\) (interquartile range 2.50--2.84). The \texttt{powerlaw} package (version 2.0.0) bounds \(\alpha\) at 3 by default, and in 8 graphs the estimate sits on that bound; the package marks these fits as invalid. With the bound lifted at the same \(x_{\min}\), their exponents lie between 3.03 and 3.39 and the median and quartiles are unchanged. Most graphs (79 of 87) therefore fall within the range 2 to 3 typical of scale-free networks, but not all." Ch8:12 "with fitted exponents between 2.1 and 3.0" → "with a median fitted exponent of 2.6". Add a footnote or one sentence to Tab. 7.2 saying that the maximum 3.00 is the package bound. Tool fix (optional, not described in the thesis): pass `parameter_ranges={'alpha': [1, None]}` and return no exponent when `fit.noise_flag` is set. See D-new2 for the Ch6 sentence that this case contradicts.

## C3. Chokepoints are read as hard to remediate, but a vulnerable chokepoint's dominated set is only its own private dependencies
- Verdict: conceded
- Evidence: `impact.py` computes `nx.immediate_dominators(G, root)` and takes the node's subtree (Ch5:240; ADR 0002). Edges point from consumer to dependency, so if v dominates w, every root→w path passes through v, and w is a descendant of v. I confirmed this for all 1,186 vulnerable chokepoints (`dominated ⊆ nx.descendants(G, v)`). Dominated-set size: median 3, Q1 1, Q3 5, and 26.8% have exactly one member. Of the vulnerable components with at least one dependency, 1,186 of 1,565 (75.8%) are chokepoints. The structural reading is the opposite of "hardest to remediate". Upgrading or replacing v also removes or upgrades its private subtree, and nothing outside that subtree loses a route to the root. Ch7 has no measure of remediation effort. The 45% and 21% figures are correct. Only the interpretation is wrong.
- Viva defence: "A vulnerable chokepoint is where one change has the most leverage. Its private dependencies arrive only through it, so upgrading or removing it settles their exposure as well. The thesis wrongly called these hard to remediate. The structural point that a flat inventory cannot show which components they are still holds."
- Minimal thesis fix: Ch7:130 "so they could not be removed or replaced without also losing those parts" → "so those parts arrive in the application only through them". Ch7:130 "These are the components for which remediation is hardest --- the ones that cannot simply be dropped --- and a flat inventory gives no indication of which they are." → "For these components a single upgrade or removal also settles the exposure of the dependencies they alone bring in, and a flat inventory gives no indication of which they are." Ch7:134 "nearly half of them are structurally irreplaceable" → "nearly half of them are the only route to some of the application's dependencies".

## C6. The top-k overlap is presented as "starker" evidence, but zero overlap is what an unrelated ranking gives at these k/N
- Verdict: partly rebutted
- Evidence: I recomputed the baseline in two ways. (a) Hypergeometric: P(overlap = 0) = C(N−k, k)/C(N, k) per SBOM, summed. (b) A permutation null with 2,000 draws per SBOM: the measure values and the script's node-ID tie-break stay fixed, and the CRITICAL labels are reassigned at random. Blast radius gives an expected 57.0 zeros out of 90 under both, an expected mean overlap of 0.134 and a median k/N of 0.117 (median k = 3, N = 25). Authority gives 51.3 zeros expected out of 79, with 54 observed. The critic's arithmetic is right, and "starker still" is wrong: a zero overlap is the typical outcome even for an unrelated ordering. One part goes further than the critic says. For blast radius, 66 zeros is more than chance predicts. Under the permutation null, P(≥ 66 zeros) = 0.008 (Poisson-binomial over the per-SBOM zero probabilities, 20,000 draws). The observed mean overlap is 0.086, against 0.134 expected. So the CRITICAL components are, if anything, less often among the largest blast radii than chance predicts. This is the same direction as the negative lean of τ_b (17 at or below −0.2 against 9 at or above 0.2). It is weak and partly tie-dependent: with ties resolved by expectation (C7), the count is 61 zeros. "Adds nothing" is therefore slightly too strong. But the overlap is not independent evidence of unrelatedness. The method (:59) defines it as a practical measure ("how many of the ones severity would point to would the structural view also point to?"), and in that role it does not need to beat chance.
- Viva defence: "The overlap is the practical consequence of τ_b ≈ 0. At three critical components out of twenty-five, an unrelated ranking gives zero overlap about two times in three, and we observe it three times in four. That is what 'unrelated' means for an analyst with limited time. It is not a second, stronger finding."
- Minimal thesis fix: Ch7:107 "The practical measure is starker still. In the median SBOM, none of …" → "The practical measure shows what this means for an analyst. In the median SBOM, none of …", and after "for authority" add: ". With so few \textsc{critical} components, this is close to what an unrelated ranking would give (an expected 57 of 90 and 51 of 79 zero overlaps), so the overlap restates the \(\tau_b\) result in practical terms rather than adding to it".

At the precision of the sentence, "between 2 and 150 milliseconds" is a rounding of 1.6–151 ms, not a misreport.
- Viva defence: "The audit's time grows with the number of packages, because it asks the registry about each one, so its cost is network latency multiplied by size. The spectral peaks are on mid-sized graphs where the backend uses a dense eigensolver. Graphs above 500 nodes switch to a sparse solver and are faster. - Minimal thesis fix: Ch7:151 "which queries the npm registry once per package; its time depends on the network rather than on the graph" → "which queries the npm registry once per package, so its time grows with the number of packages and depends on network latency". ## C15. "Negligible" for |τ_b| < 0.2 is presented as a convention without a source, and τ_b itself is uncited
- Verdict: conceded (the critic's own threshold is also unsourced)
- Evidence: `Bibliography.bib` has no Kendall entry and no effect-size scale. Counts: |τ_b| < 0.2 in 64 of 90 (blast) and 52 of 79 (authority, not stated in Ch7), and |τ_b| < 0.1 in 38 and 28. The critic's "common scales put negligible below 0.1" is also given without a source. I could not open Schober et al. (2018), the usual reference for a 0.00–0.10 "negligible" band (paywall, HTTP 402), so I do not rely on it. A source-free check: for bivariate normal data, Kendall's τ and Pearson's r are related by r = sin(πτ/2) (Greiner's relation). τ = 0.2 then corresponds to r ≈ 0.31, which most scales call weak-to-moderate, not negligible. So the 0.2 cut is generous for τ. τ_b = 1 ceiling: with the actual band ties and blast values sorted concordantly, the maximum attainable τ_b has median 0.79 (minimum 0.47), so the gloss at :59 is not exact. This does not affect the reading of τ_b ≈ 0.
- Viva defence: "The conclusion rests on the median, 0.00, and the interquartile range, −0.17 to 0.11, not on a label. 'Small' is the defensible word."
- Minimal thesis fix: Ch7:107 "64 of the 90 have \(|\tau_b| < 0.2\), a level conventionally read as negligible association" → "64 of the 90 have \(|\tau_b| < 0.2\) and 38 have \(|\tau_b| < 0.1\)".
