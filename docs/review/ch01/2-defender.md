# Chapter 1 and Abstract — Defender

All code references are at `28113e7` (`git show 28113e7:<path>`). Recomputations were run on `sbom-lens-eval/analysis/out/per_sbom.csv` (90 SBOMs) with the venv Python (`sbom-lens/venv/bin/python`, csv and statistics only): median transitive-only share 0.75, median direct share 0.115, median τ_b 0.003 (blast radius, 90) and −0.004 (authority, 79), median nodes 332. These match the critic and the Abstract. I re-read every Ch1 and Abstract line the critic cites and the Ch2–Ch8 lines it compares them with. External facts were checked by web search: OSSRA 2024 (96 % of 1,067 codebases contain open source, 77 % of the code is open source, 526 components on average), CRA Art. 3(39) (an SBOM is "a formal record containing details and supply chain relationships of components"), and the September 2025 npm compromises (chalk/debug on 8 September; Shai-Hulud from 15 September, more than 500 packages, CISA alert of 23 September). The critic is right that Ch1 was not updated with the rest. It omits dominance, paths, the RQs and the npm sample, and it still has a few normative and universal sentences that Ch3, Ch7 and Ch8 have since qualified. Most items need only a few words. None of them touches a result, and no item reopens a decision already taken. Where Ch1 motivates or simplifies without contradicting a later chapter, I defend it.

- Minimal thesis fix: :41 RO2, after "and at what severity" insert ", and, for any vulnerable component, to compute its blast radius, the dependency paths through which it enters the application, and its dominated set". :42 RO3 delete "impact traversal to enumerate all components affected by a vulnerability in a given node, ". :54 Contribution 2, after "carry known vulnerabilities." insert "For any vulnerable component, SBOM Lens computes its blast radius, up to five shortest dependency paths to it, and its dominated set --- the components that could not be reached without it." :55 Contribution 3 delete "Impact traversal identifies all components that depend --- directly or transitively --- on a given vulnerable node, making the blast radius of any vulnerability immediately visible." :71 "articulation point detection, centrality analysis, and community detection are well-established techniques" → "articulation point detection, dominator trees, centrality analysis, and community detection are well-established techniques". ## C5. Ch1:9 says a transitive dependency is "invisible" without a graph model, but a flat inventory lists transitive components; what it hides is how they entered
- Verdict: conceded
- Evidence: The preceding sentence ("The difficulty organisations faced in determining whether they were exposed") is supported by `GoogleLog4j2021` (bib :531). That post's point is that most affected artifacts used log4j indirectly, often deep in the chain, which made exposure hard to establish and slow to fix. The conclusion "remains invisible until it is exploited" is too strong, however. Any flat SBOM or SCA scan (grype, Ch3:16) lists log4j-core, and Ch7:128 and the Ch8 C2 sort settled that what a flat list hides is how a component entered and what depends on it. "Until it is exploited" has no source.
- Viva defence: a flat inventory tells you that log4j is present. It does not tell you through which of your dependencies it arrived, or what else is exposed. That is what the graph adds.
- Minimal thesis fix: :9 "without a structural model of the dependency graph, the presence of a transitive dependency remains invisible until it is exploited." → "without a structural model of the dependency graph, an organisation may see that a vulnerable transitive dependency is present, but not how it entered the system or what depends on it."

## C8. "Accurate and complete picture of the blast radius" contradicts the disclosed over-approximation and lower bounds
- Verdict: partly rebutted

Over-approximation (Ch7:157, Ch8:45) concerns code-level exposure, which Ch1 never claims. Lower bounds (Ch7:159) come from generator errors, not from SBOM Lens. - Viva defence: SBOM Lens computes the blast radius exactly over the dependencies the SBOM records. How faithful the SBOM is, and whether the vulnerable code executes, are separate limitations, stated in Ch7 and Ch8.
- Minimal thesis fix: :31 "the system immediately identifies all components that depend on it, at any depth, giving the analyst an accurate and complete picture of the blast radius." → "the system identifies all components that depend on it, at any depth --- its blast radius, as far as the SBOM records the dependencies --- together with the dependency paths that lead to it and the components that could be reached only through it." ## C12. The CRA sentence in Ch1:11 glosses the SBOM as covering "all software components and their dependency relationships" and says it "has transitioned" to a legal requirement; Ch2 says "top-level dependencies" and "becoming"
